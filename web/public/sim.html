Ledify sound simulator

The board's own sound pipeline, ported line for line from mic.c, fft.c and fx.c, fed by a simulated ICS-43434 microphone built from its datasheet. The strip below is what the LEDs would show, frame by frame, at the firmware's frame rate.

Sound in

Room level is the sound pressure at the board, as the long-term RMS of the signal. For a file or this device's mic, it is the level that a −20 dBFS input stands for, so the dynamics of the recording come through. 60 dB is talking, 80 a loud speaker across the room, 100 a club.

Measured in simulated time from each drum hit in the test signals to the moment its frame has finished going down the wire. Without prediction a beat can only be lit after it is heard: the 8 ms sample block, then the frame, then the wire. With prediction on, once the tempo is steady (lock 30 or more) the board fires each beat early by the time it takes to light the LEDs, so they land on the hit. Wi-Fi plays no part when the board's own mic drives the LEDs.

Data formatI²S, 24-bit two's complement, 64 SCK per frameSamples are rounded to 24 bits and handed to the same code as raw[i] >> 8 on the board.

Not modelled: the mic's frequency-response curve between 60 Hz and 20 kHz (taken as flat), the acoustic port and the PCB hole in front of it, THD below the clip point (0.2 % at 105 dB SPL), and supply noise (PSR −99 dBFS, far below anything the styles react to). The browser's own mic and speakers come in and go out through your device, so “Hear it” and the mic source run with their extra delay; the latency numbers are the board's, in simulated time.
